# Round 2: Godin lens (Audience and Meaning)

_Lens inspired by Seth Godin's public writing. Paraphrase only, no quotes._

## 1. Where I've been persuaded

- **Graham and Isenberg: the paid workaround.** My ladder jumped from "verbatim pain" to "a card I'd sign." They're right that a rung belongs in between: someone already pays a fixer, an agency or a Fastwork gig for this. Pain alone is noise. A workaround someone pays for shows the problem matters to them.
- **Hormozi and Levels: money is the cleanest permission.** A deposit through Stripe or Opn says more than ten warm conversations. I'll put the payment rail ahead of my own favourite, LINE OA.
- **Walling: LINE is a platform risk.** I called the LINE OA "the asset," but the asset is the relationship, not the platform. Mirror the list in email and keep it exportable. Retention by day 90 is the honest test of whether people actually trusted you.
- **Graham: don't gate on 80–90% at day 1.** Trust-bearing work is human at first. Measure at month 6, not at the card.
- **Isenberg: no agent may promote an idea it generated.** That's a rule about trust inside the machine, and I'm adopting it.

## 2. Where I still disagree, and why

- **Levels: a bot posting to subreddits, Pantip and LINE OpenChat.** That's the step where the factory turns into spam. Isenberg's own list says bots get banned from these places. In a Thai OpenChat, one screenshot of a bot post costs face the founder can't buy back. The agent drafts the post. The founder posts it under his own name, or it doesn't go out.
- **Hormozi: 50 cold DMs and 10 ad-funded tests a month.** Paid ads to strangers mostly find people who click. Hormozi's own kill rule 2 says paid CAC kills most ฿99–199 B2C ideas, which is my point: if the business only works on bought attention, it isn't agent-operable, because the cost just moves into the ad budget. Ads can confirm demand. They can't stand in for an audience.
- **Sanchez: HN as an anti-signal, and agents doing the outreach to owners.** Crowded is a fair worry. But a crowd of builders is also where Yaru's own students are. And an agent cold-emailing an ageing business owner about selling is the fastest way to lose his trust.
- **Walling: "audience-first adds lag and platform dependence."** Every channel is a platform, SEO included. The fix is an owned, portable list, not having no audience. And the founder already has one (his students, professionals building with AI), so the lag is near zero.
- **The whole panel: "Thais and expats" as one audience.** Nearly every paper still mixes them. They're two tribes, with different trust networks, languages and status games. Every card should pick one.

## 3. The one thing the final system must not lose

**A named group of people who would miss the product, reached by a human they recognise.**

Agents can mine, cluster, build and bill. What they can't do is be someone the reader trusts. So every surviving idea carries a change card: "This is for ___ who want ___; it helps them get ___," naming a group the founder can reach this week under his own name. And the loop tracks one thing beyond revenue: **does anyone tell a friend?** If more automation brings fewer referrals, the business isn't getting more efficient. It's getting cheaper while it fades.
